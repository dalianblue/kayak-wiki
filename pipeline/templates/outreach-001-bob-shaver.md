**Subject: Permission to use your Aleut kayak build photos — a Chinese kayak heritage project**

Dear Mr. Shaver,

I'm a sea kayaker and paddler based in Shanghai, China. This summer I started building a
knowledge archive about the history and evolution of the kayak — from the Greenland qajaq
and the Aleut baidarka, through folding kayaks and the British sea kayak revolution, to
the boats we paddle today. The site is here: https://fxmlab.top/kayak/ (in Chinese).

Why we're doing this: Chinese-language kayak communities are almost entirely built on
short social-media posts. The forums that once carried long-form knowledge have mostly
disappeared. Meanwhile, the deep knowledge of skin-on-frame traditions lives almost
exclusively in English. We want to build the first serious Chinese-language archive of
kayak design lineage — so that a beginner in China can trace today's boats back to the
Aleutian and Greenland hunters who invented them.

I found your article "Building an Aleut Kayak" and spent a long time with it. Your photos
of the forked bow construction — the lashed pieces, the comparison with the traditional
baidarka bow — show exactly what written description cannot. In Chinese, there is simply
nothing like it.

May I have your permission to use 3–4 photos from that article on our site? To be specific:

- We would use them only in the "Aleut baidarka" section, illustrating forked-bow
  construction in a skin-on-frame build;
- Each photo would carry a clear credit line: "Photo: Bob Shaver,
  backpackingtechnology.com" with a link to your original article;
- Use is strictly non-commercial — the site has no advertising and no revenue;
- If you prefer a specific license (e.g., CC BY-NC), we will follow exactly what you say;
- I'm happy to send you the page for your review before it goes live, and to take
  anything down at any time, no questions asked.

I should also say: your About page reads like a life well spent in the mountains —
Sierra peaks in the late '60s, nordic ski patrol, mountain rescue, twelve years teaching
backpacking at Boise State. Our project exists precisely because people like you have
always passed this knowledge on. I'd be honored to pass a piece of yours to Chinese readers.

Thank you for your time, and for sharing your build in the first place.

With respect,
